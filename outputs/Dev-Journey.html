Development Retrospective · Jan–Aug 2026

Seven months, three repos, one question: which card should I use?

Monet is a credit card rewards optimizer. You search a merchant, and it tells you which card in your wallet to pull out — the one that earns the most for that purchase. I built it solo across a SwiftUI iOS app, a serverless TypeScript backend on AWS, and an Astro PWA web client. This is the story told through 680 commits, 195 days, and 78,934 net lines of code across three submodules.

I started on January 23, 2026 with an empty Xcode project and a hypothesis: if I could categorize a merchant correctly, I could tell you which credit card earns the most. Seven months later, Monet spans an iOS native app, a AWS serverless backend, and an Astro PWA web client. It features Plaid bank linking, location-aware address resolution, Bilt housing calculators, RankedSpend optimization, guest mode with certificate pinning, and an automated merchant categorization audit engine with HTML alerts.

Act I "Can AI tell me which card to use?" Jan – Feb 2026

The iOS repo's first real commit after the scaffold was blunt: init categorizer using gemini api (9a2d5cd, Jan 24). Four days later I pivoted: use local api instead of gemini api (68970df). The backend was born the same week: an Express-style Node server with a /categorize endpoint (e7c0635, Jan 28), then a TypeScript migration on Feb 7.

February is when the product found a voice. On Feb 18 I added Google Sign-In, switched to Apple MLKSearch for on-device categorization, and moved card recommendations local: hook card reccomendation based on category locally, remove reccomendation api call (e6084d6). Then came Feb 22: nineteen commits in a single day. Places API integration, fuzzy brand matching, a sunset-themed UI overhaul (400a5c5), quick search chips, card nicknames, and an APIClient refactor that deleted 779 lines of local categorization code (e0c9bd4).

Two days later, on Feb 23, the backend absorbed what the phone had been doing locally: transfer brand categorizer and data from iOS app to backend (b9b28d7, +3,543 / −429 lines). Card catalogs moved server-side. The categorization brain trust now lived in one place, and the phone went back to doing what phones do well: showing things to a human.

Act II "Connect the bank, see the damage" Mar – Apr 2026

March was quieter (12 iOS commits, 6 backend) but foundational. I added network monitoring and offline UI on the app side (2e37ce8), and on Mar 9 both repos landed their first Plaid integration within hours of each other: Link flow on iOS (08d7222), endpoints on croe (5b8003c, 01a26ac).

April is where things got messy and productive at the same time. Apr 4 brought InsightsView and a major navigation restructure on iOS (3e24961, +866 lines) while the backend expanded Plaid with link tokens and DynamoDB transaction storage (95a0200). Then, on Apr 15 at 00:50, I deleted Plaid from the iOS app entirely:

May brought a design-system explosion: swiftui-pro first pass (384e3c2), a premium animated LoginView (bb752ce), MonetListItem and MonetCard components, iOS 18 TabView syntax, and the largest single refactor in iOS history (a17d46b: +1,174 / −1,305 lines, 15+ components extracted). Gemini was fully excised from both repos (8f2f436 iOS, 5cbd76f backend). Bilt 2.0 Blueprint landed on both sides (533499c, 58f9903).

Act IV "Make the optimizer smart, then ship beta" May 27 – 31, 2026

The final week of May was a blitz. Backend: AWS Bedrock for unknown merchants (0dd07e4, 5f3aa87), RankedSpend optimization (4ac3214), O(N) scan to O(1) index lookup for Plaid webhooks (6f2fe97), guest JWT auth and rate limiting, and an opus 4.8 security review pass (c0bf3ae, 28 files). iOS: 946 lines of unit tests (820d0d8), incremental Plaid sync (60a1277), guest mode with custom AuthService (926c752, +1,007 lines), SSL certificate pinning (cd31565), and guest-to-account merge bug fixes.

Act V "Hardening, Swift 6 Concurrency & Cloud Native Observability" June 2026

June focused on system resilience and developer experience across 131 commits. iOS migrated to complete Swift 6 strict concurrency compliance (4a452b6), centralized caching configurations inside AppConfig (a3051cf), and added a feedback composer with in-app log file export (94a7637). On-device telemetry expanded with TelemetryDeck integration, capturing locale, timezone, session length, and query strings.

Act VI "The AIRLINE Split, App Widgets & Web Demo PWA Launch" July 2026

July brought major user-facing expansions across 74 commits. A critical category domain model overhaul introduced the dedicated AIRLINE category on both iOS (2a3ea04) and backend (db86cd9), separating flight purchases from general travel to accurately model multipliers like Amex Platinum 5x on flights vs. Chase Sapphire 3x on broad travel.

Meanwhile, the website underwent a full rewrite, migrating from Next.js to Astro v5 (f8496c7) to deliver a zero-friction, lightning-fast PWA experience (d91d588, ec4d5c3). The web demo featured an interactive mock iOS lock screen hero video (a34d400), dynamic card catalog fetching from live API endpoints (44fc37f), GA4 referral tracking (f450de0), and offline recommendations with local fallback. On iOS, WidgetCenter support landed with App Groups persistence for lock screen and home screen widgets (a83edae).

Act VII "Location-Aware Intelligence, Category Provenance & Bilt Housing" August 1 – 5, 2026

The first week of August delivered Monet's most sophisticated intelligence updates across 66 commits. To prevent convenience store gas stations (e.g. 7-Eleven or Wawa) from misclassifying in-store snack purchases as 3% Gas rewards, the backend and iOS app added exact address-based resolution (a566769, 466aa43) and explicit category provenance tracking (categorySource). Mixed-use convenience brands were reclassified to neutral OTHER categories, while PlacesCache gained TTL and LRU pruning (3566505).

"feat: add biltCalculator module to optimize everyday spend and housing rewards scenarios"

8520436 · croe · 2026-08-03 · +482 lines

Backend and iOS simultaneously launched the Bilt Housing Calculator (8520436, 5202709), tracking rent point caps and evaluating everyday spend matching requirements (5 transactions/month rule). Finally, a backend transaction categorization audit system landed with 90-day TTL records and automated HTML review emails (0c0d44c, 531e0ff), while raw MCC reliance was officially retired in favor of high-fidelity merchant resolution.

Aha Moments

Both Exact Address Resolution & Category Provenance

Aug 5: Introduced exact address resolution and explicit categorySource metadata (a566769, 466aa43). Mixed-use convenience stores (like 7-Eleven gas locations) were reclassified to neutral OTHER categories to prevent false 3% Gas reward recommendations for non-fuel purchases.

Lesson: Merchant string matching alone fails when brands span multiple purchase types. Context-dependent location resolution and explicit category provenance tracking prevent incorrect financial recommendations.

Website The Astro v5 Zero-Friction PWA Wedge

Jul 15: Migrated landing and demo app from Next.js to Astro v5 (f8496c7). Implemented service worker offline caching, live API dynamic catalog loading, and an interactive mock iOS lock screen hero video (a34d400).

Lesson: A ultra-fast, zero-install web application acts as an instant conversion funnel. Users test Monet's recommendation engine in browser before downloading the native iOS app.

Both The AIRLINE Category Domain Split

Jul 25: Created dedicated AIRLINE category across backend (db86cd9) and iOS app (2a3ea04), separating flights from broad travel.

Lesson: Premium cards treat flights differently than general transit or hotels. Broad travel categories caused suboptimal recommendations for cards with flight-specific bonus tiers (e.g. Amex Platinum 5x).

Backend O(N) → O(1) Webhook Lookup & Metric Math

Jun–Jul: Replaced full table scans with MonetPlaidItemIndex lookups (6f2fe97). Consolidated CloudWatch alarms using metric math expressions (081ff42) to stay within AWS Free Tier limits.

Lesson: Production serverless backends must optimize both data access patterns and cloud monitoring costs. High-fidelity metric math keeps operational visibility high and costs low.

iOS Swift 6 Strict Concurrency & In-App Feedback Logs

Jun 15: Upgraded iOS app to Swift 6 concurrency rules (4a452b6). Built a feedback composer that packages sanitized diagnostic logs and session state directly into email/share attachments (94a7637).

Lesson: Compile-time thread safety combined with friction-free user log exports makes debugging offline edge cases significantly faster.

Both The Plaid Yo-Yo

Mar 9: first integration. Apr 4: Insights built on Plaid. Apr 15: remove plaid (−975 iOS, −598 backend). Apr 24: add plaid on both repos, rebuilt through DataStore and modular routes.

Lesson: Not a revert. A deliberate teardown to introduce persistence-first architecture, then reintegration nine days later with multi-item support and webhooks.

Backend Gemini → Bedrock + Categorization Audit Engine

May 28: AWS Bedrock integration for unknown merchants (0dd07e4). Aug 3: Automated transaction categorization audit system with 90-day TTL records and HTML email review tables (0c0d44c).

Lesson: Staying AWS-native for AI keeps latency minimal, while automated audit tables ensure human oversight of edge-case merchant categorizations.

Lessons Learned

  • Location context beats brand strings. A convenience store selling snacks shouldn't offer 3% Gas rewards just because the parent brand owns gas pumps. Exact address resolution and provenance metadata are required.
  • Delete bravely, rebuild intentionally. Deleting Plaid in April enabled SwiftData architecture; replacing raw MCCs in August enabled audit-backed location intelligence. High deletion months reflect architecting for longevity.
  • Submodules require synchronized discipline. Schema updates, category renames (e.g., AIRLINE), and Bilt calculator logic landed across iOS, backend, and web submodules within hours of each other.
  • Zero-install web experiences drive native engagement. An Astro-powered PWA with service worker caching lets users test recommendation logic instantly, boosting trust before app installation.
  • Security and observability reviews surface real bugs. Automated 90-day audit tables, CloudWatch metric math, and in-app log exporting surfaced edge cases far faster than manual testing.
  • Rest fuels momentum. Taking time off in mid-May was followed by major blitzes: RankedSpend in late May, Swift 6 in June, Astro PWA in July, and Address Resolution in August.

Where This Leaves Monet

680 commits later, Monet is an ecosystem: 22,040 lines of Swift on iOS, 19,233 lines of TypeScript on backend, 1,895 lines of Astro on web, location-aware address resolution, Bilt housing calculators, guest mode with SSL certificate pinning, and an audit-backed optimizer that understands quarterly bonuses, spend caps, and RankedSpend tier assignment.

The impressionist name fits. Up close, the history looks like continuous iteration: schema evolutions, category domain splits, and address resolution refinements. Step back, and a complete system emerges: a financial rewards engine built with craft, precision, and relentless shipping.
